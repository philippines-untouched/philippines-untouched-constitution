# Chapter 33 — Traceability

*This chapter defines complete product traceability from origin to customer as a core organisational capability. The Company can account for every product it makes — where it came from, what happened to it, and where it went.*

---

## 33.1 Traceability Is Foundational

Complete traceability is a foundational principle of the Company.

The Company shall maintain records covering the full lifecycle of its products:

> **Seed / planting material → cultivation → harvest → processing → packaging → storage → distribution → customer**

The Company can follow any product forward from its origin and backward from its destination. This capability is not an afterthought; it is built into the way the Company works.

---

## 33.2 What Traceability Supports

Complete traceability is not an end in itself. It serves:

- **Food safety** — the ability to act swiftly and precisely when a safety concern arises;
- **Product authenticity** — proof that a product is what the Company says it is;
- **Quality assurance** — the evidence that standards were met at every stage;
- **Customer confidence** — the assurance that customers can trust what they receive;
- **Export compliance** — the records that international markets require;
- **Organisational learning** — the understanding of the Company's own history.

---

## 33.3 The Discipline of Traceability

The Company's traceability shall be:

- **Complete** — covering every stage of the product's journey;
- **Accurate** — reflecting what actually happened;
- **Permanent** — preserved for as long as it may be needed;
- **Verifiable** — capable of being examined and relied upon;
- **Connected** — linking each stage to the next, so that no product is lost between records.

---

## 33.4 Systems and Customers

Digital systems shall support the Company's traceability capability.

Where appropriate, mechanisms such as QR codes may be used to expose traceability information to customers — sharing the product's story honestly, while protecting what must remain confidential.

The information offered to customers shall be accurate and complete enough to be trusted, and presented in a manner customers can understand.

---

## 33.5 Traceability and the Company's Memory

Traceability is part of the Company's organisational memory.

The records of the Company's products — where they came from, how they were made and where they went — are part of the institutional knowledge preserved for future generations. The Company's traceability is therefore both a service to its customers and an investment in its own history.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **The Company can account for every product from origin to customer — completely, accurately and permanently — as a core capability serving safety, trust and learning.**
