# Chapter 15 — Ethical Leadership

*This chapter establishes the standards of integrity expected from all leaders of the Company. Leadership is stewardship: those who lead the Company are entrusted with its direction, its people and its reputation, and shall exercise that trust with the highest standards of integrity.*

---

## 15.1 Leadership Is Stewardship

Those who lead the Company hold a position of trust.

Leadership is not the exercise of personal power; it is the stewardship of the Company's purpose, its people, its resources and its reputation. A leader of the Company is a custodian of what has been entrusted to the Company, serving the Company's enduring interests rather than personal ones.

---

## 15.2 The Standards of Leadership

All leaders of the Company — directors, executives, officers and managers — shall:

- **Act with integrity** — honestly, consistently and in accordance with the Company's Core Values and Founding Principles;
- **Lead by example** — demonstrating in their own conduct the standards they expect of others;
- **Serve the Company's purpose** — placing the Company's enduring interests above personal advantage;
- **Exercise authority responsibly** — using delegated authority for the Company's benefit, never for private benefit;
- **Be accountable** — answering openly for their decisions and their exercise of authority;
- **Decide on evidence** — making significant decisions on trustworthy information, with recorded reasons;
- **Treat people with respect** — honouring the dignity and contribution of every person;
- **Protect the Company's reputation** — conducting themselves so that the Company's good name is strengthened;
- **Prepare for continuity** — developing successors and ensuring the Company can continue without them.

---

## 15.3 Conflict of Interest

Leaders shall avoid conflicts between their personal interests and the Company's interests.

Where a conflict exists or may arise, it shall be disclosed promptly and handled in the Company's interests, in accordance with the Company's governance.

A leader shall not use their position to obtain private benefit at the Company's expense, nor permit personal relationships to compromise the Company's decisions.

---

## 15.4 The Limits of Leadership

Leadership does not place anyone above the Company's principles.

No leader — including the founder, the owner or the chief executive — is above the Constitution, the law or the Company's governing standards. The authority of leadership is bounded by the principles it serves.

---

## 15.5 Leadership and the Future

The quality of the Company's future depends upon the quality of its leadership.

The Company shall develop leaders who understand its principles and are capable of upholding them, and shall ensure that leadership passes on in an orderly manner. A leader who serves the Company well leaves it stronger, better led and ready for those who follow.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **Leadership is stewardship. Those who lead the Company shall act with integrity, serve the Company's purpose, and uphold its principles above all personal interest.**
