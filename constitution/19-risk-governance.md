# Chapter 19 — Risk Governance

*This chapter explains how risk should be understood, managed and balanced against opportunity. The Company does not avoid risk; it governs risk — so that the Company may pursue its purpose with confidence, without endangering its future.*

---

## 19.1 Understanding Risk

Risk is the possibility that events or decisions may lead to outcomes different from those intended — including harm to the Company's people, its reputation, its finances, its land or its future.

Risk is not, by itself, something to be feared. Every worthwhile undertaking carries risk. The Company's duty is to understand the risks it faces, govern them deliberately, and balance them against the opportunities they accompany.

---

## 19.2 The Principles of Risk Governance

The Company's approach to risk rests on these principles:

- **Understanding** — risks shall be identified and understood, not ignored;
- **Assessment** — risks shall be assessed for their likelihood and their consequences;
- **Proportion** — the effort of managing a risk shall be proportionate to its significance;
- **Balance** — risk shall be balanced against opportunity, not avoided at any cost;
- **Stewardship** — risks that could harm the Company's long-term endurance shall be treated with particular care;
- **Learning** — the Company shall learn from risk events and near-misses;
- **Openness** — significant risks and their management shall be reported through the Company's governance.

---

## 19.3 Managing Risk

The Company shall manage risk deliberately:

- **Identify** — recognise the risks inherent in the Company's activities and decisions;
- **Assess** — understand the likelihood and consequences of each significant risk;
- **Respond** — decide how each risk shall be handled: avoided, reduced, shared, accepted or managed;
- **Monitor** — review risks and their management over time, as circumstances change;
- **Review** — examine the outcomes of risk decisions so that the Company learns.

---

## 19.4 Risk and Opportunity

Risk and opportunity are two faces of the same decisions.

The Company shall not pursue opportunity while ignoring the risk it carries, nor decline opportunity out of fear of risk it has not assessed. Each significant decision shall weigh both, in accordance with the Decision Framework.

The Company's purpose is not served by recklessness, nor by timidity. It is served by judgement — the disciplined evaluation of what may be gained, what may be lost, and what the Company can bear.

---

## 19.5 Risk and the Company's Future

The greatest risks to the Company are those that could threaten its endurance: risks to its reputation, its integrity, its people, its land and its principles.

The Company shall protect what it cannot afford to lose. Where a risk could compromise the Company's integrity or its founding principles, the risk shall not be accepted, whatever the potential reward.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **The Company governs risk with understanding and judgement — balancing risk against opportunity, learning from experience, and never accepting a risk that would compromise its integrity or its future.**
