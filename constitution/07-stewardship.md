# Chapter 7 — Stewardship

*This chapter defines the Company's most fundamental duty: to hold in trust everything entrusted to it — the land, the people, the Company itself and the knowledge it acquires — and to leave each of them stronger for future generations.*

---

## 7.1 The Duty of Stewardship

The Company is a steward, not merely an owner.

Everything the Company holds — its land, its resources, its people, its knowledge, its reputation and the Company itself — is entrusted to the current generation for the benefit of those who follow.

The duty of stewardship is expressed in the principle:

> **Each generation shall leave the Company, its land, its people and its knowledge stronger than it found them.**

---

## 7.2 Stewardship of the Land

Land is not merely a commercial asset. It is something entrusted to the Company.

The Company shall seek to:

- **Protect soil health** — so that the land remains productive for generations;
- **Protect biodiversity** — so that the natural life of the land flourishes;
- **Protect waterways** — so that water remains clean and abundant;
- **Use resources responsibly** — consuming only what is needed, and using it well;
- **Reduce waste** — avoiding waste in every activity, from field to customer;
- **Improve the land over time** — leaving it more fertile, not less;
- **Preserve natural ecosystems** — protecting the living systems the Company depends upon;
- **Build climate resilience** — preparing the land and the Company for environmental change;
- **Use renewable energy where practical** — reducing dependence on resources that cannot be renewed.

The guiding principle for the land:

> **Every generation shall leave the land healthier than it found it.**

---

## 7.3 Stewardship of People

Stewardship extends to the people who contribute to the Company's success.

The Company shall treat its people with fairness, dignity and respect; invest in their development; and ensure that those who contribute share appropriately in the value they help create.

A Company that consumes its people without investing in them has failed in its duty of stewardship.

---

## 7.4 Stewardship of the Company

The Company is itself an entrusted asset — an institution to be preserved and strengthened for future generations.

The current generation shall govern the Company soundly, manage its resources responsibly, protect its reputation and maintain its financial strength, so that the Company is passed on in a stronger condition than it was received.

Stewardship of the Company includes the orderly transfer of leadership, so that the Company never depends upon any single individual.

---

## 7.5 Stewardship of Knowledge

The knowledge the Company acquires — its practices, its records, its lessons and its history — is part of what is entrusted to each generation.

The Company shall preserve its knowledge, record its decisions and their reasons, and pass what it has learned to those who follow.

Knowledge that is not preserved is a stewardship failure: the Company would be forced to relearn, generation after generation, what it had already learned.

---

## 7.6 The Measure of Stewardship

The success of each generation's stewardship is measured not by what it takes from the Company, the land or the people, but by what it leaves behind.

Stewardship is fulfilled when those who follow inherit:

- land that is healthier;
- people who are more capable and more fairly rewarded;
- a Company that is stronger, better governed and more trusted;
- knowledge that is preserved and shared;
- and a reputation that endures.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **The Company is a steward, not merely an owner. Each generation shall leave the land, the people, the Company and the knowledge entrusted to it stronger than it found them.**
