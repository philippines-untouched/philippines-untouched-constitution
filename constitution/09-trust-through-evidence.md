# Chapter 9 — Trust Through Evidence

*This chapter establishes accurate information, traceability and evidence-based decision making as foundations of the Company's trust. The Company's word is backed by its records, and its records shall be trustworthy.*

---

## 9.1 Trust Is Built on Evidence

The Company earns trust through consistent, honest and verifiable conduct.

Trust that rests only on words is fragile. The Company's trust rests on evidence: accurate information, complete records and traceable history that others may examine and rely upon.

The Company's philosophy is:

> **There can be no better quality control than trustworthy evidence preserved over time.**

---

## 9.2 The Principles of Evidence

The Company's approach to evidence rests on these principles:

- **Accuracy** — records shall reflect what actually happened, not what was convenient to believe;
- **Integrity** — records shall be complete, consistent and protected from alteration;
- **Immutability** — once recorded, important evidence shall not be silently changed or erased;
- **Traceability** — the history of products, decisions and activities shall be capable of being followed;
- **Auditability** — the Company's records shall be open to verification, by the Company and by those entitled to examine them;
- **Versioning** — when records change, the history of change shall itself be preserved;
- **Reproducibility** — an outcome shall be capable of being understood and, where appropriate, repeated;
- **Preservation** — important evidence shall endure for as long as it may be needed.

Technology may change. The principle of trustworthy evidence shall not.

---

## 9.3 Evidence-Based Decision Making

The Company shall make its important decisions on the basis of evidence, not assumption.

Decisions shall be informed by accurate information, examined for what the evidence shows, and recorded with their reasons, so that future generations can understand not only what was decided but why.

The absence of evidence is itself a fact to be weighed. The Company shall not treat assumption as fact, nor assertion as proof.

---

## 9.4 Important Activities Leave Evidence

Important decisions and important business activities shall leave sufficient evidence for future generations to understand what happened and, where appropriate, why it happened.

This principle applies to:

- **Product history** — the journey of products from origin to customer;
- **Business decisions** — significant decisions and the reasoning behind them;
- **Quality and safety records** — the verification that standards were met;
- **Financial records** — the honest accounting of the Company's affairs;
- **Organisational memory** — the knowledge and lessons the Company accumulates.

---

## 9.5 Evidence and Trust

The Company's evidence is a promise to those who rely upon it: customers, regulators, partners, auditors, investors and future generations.

A customer should be able to trust what the Company says about its products because the Company can show it. A future leader should be able to understand past decisions because the Company recorded them. A regulator should find the Company's records in order because the Company keeps them in order.

The Company shall never present as evidence something it knows to be false, and shall never conceal or destroy evidence to avoid accountability.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **The Company earns trust through trustworthy evidence: accurate, complete, preserved and verifiable.**
