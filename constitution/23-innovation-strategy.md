# Chapter 23 — Innovation Strategy

*This chapter explains how innovation should solve meaningful problems rather than create unnecessary complexity. The Company's innovation serves its purpose — improving quality, strengthening stewardship and building enduring value.*

---

## 23.1 Innovation Serves Purpose

The Company's commitment to innovation is a commitment to improvement.

Innovation shall serve the Company's purpose: solving meaningful problems, improving quality, strengthening trust, protecting nature and building enduring value. Innovation that does not serve the Company's purpose — however novel — is not innovation the Company needs.

---

## 23.2 Meaningful Problems

The Company innovates to solve meaningful problems.

The Company's innovation shall be directed at the real challenges of its work: producing better food, farming more sustainably, protecting quality and evidence, serving customers more honestly and building the Company's capability.

The Company shall seek to understand the problem before pursuing a solution, in accordance with its whole-system principle. Innovation begins with understanding, not with novelty.

---

## 23.3 Innovation Without Unnecessary Complexity

Innovation shall simplify where it can, and add complexity only where complexity creates value.

The Company shall:

- prefer the simplest solution that fully solves the problem;
- adopt innovation for the value it creates, not for its novelty;
- remove complexity that does not serve the Company's purpose;
- ensure that new practices, systems and products are understandable, maintainable and capable of being handed on.

An innovation that burdens the Company with complexity it does not need is not an improvement; it is a cost disguised as progress.

---

## 23.4 The Discipline of Innovation

The Company's innovation shall be disciplined:

- **Purposeful** — directed at meaningful problems, not pursued for its own sake;
- **Evidence-based** — tested against evidence, not adopted on fashion or assumption;
- **Gradual** — introduced in steps that can be understood, verified and corrected;
- **Consistent** — never at the cost of quality, safety, integrity or the Company's standards;
- **Shared** — knowledge of what works, and what does not, shall be captured and passed on.

---

## 23.5 Innovation and Continuous Improvement

Innovation includes the steady improvement of daily work, not only the introduction of the new.

The Company shall improve continually — refining its practices, learning from its experience and applying what it learns. Large innovations and small improvements are both expressions of the same commitment: to do the Company's work better, in service of its purpose.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **Innovation must serve purpose. The Company innovates to solve meaningful problems, simplifies wherever it can, and adds complexity only where it creates value.**
