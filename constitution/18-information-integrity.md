# Chapter 18 — Information Integrity

*This chapter defines information as a strategic asset of the Company, requiring accuracy, security and permanence. The Company's decisions, its trust and its memory all depend upon the integrity of its information.*

---

## 18.1 Information Is a Strategic Asset

Information is one of the Company's most valuable assets — as valuable as its land, its equipment and its reputation.

The Company's information includes its records, its evidence, its knowledge, its decisions and its history. This information supports the Company's decisions, sustains its trust, and preserves its memory for future generations.

Because information is a strategic asset, its integrity shall be protected with the same care as any asset of the Company.

---

## 18.2 The Integrity of Information

The Company's information shall be:

- **Accurate** — reflecting what actually happened, not what was convenient to believe;
- **Complete** — not missing what is needed to understand the truth;
- **Secure** — protected from unauthorised access, alteration or destruction;
- **Permanent** — preserved for as long as it may be needed, including across generations;
- **Verifiable** — capable of being examined and relied upon;
- **Traceable** — connected to the activities and decisions they record.

Information that is inaccurate, incomplete or insecure cannot support the Company's decisions or its trust. The Company shall therefore protect the integrity of its information as a first responsibility, not an afterthought.

---

## 18.3 Information and Decision Making

The Company's decisions are only as sound as the information upon which they rest.

The Company shall ensure that significant decisions are supported by information of known integrity — accurate, current and complete — and that the reasoning behind decisions is recorded with the decisions themselves.

---

## 18.4 Security and Access

The Company's information shall be secured according to its sensitivity and importance.

The Company shall:

- protect confidential and personal information from unauthorised access;
- control access to information according to responsibility and need;
- protect information against loss, corruption and unauthorised alteration;
- maintain the availability of information to those who legitimately require it;
- comply with the laws and obligations governing the information it holds.

Security is not the enemy of transparency. The Company protects what must be protected so that it can honestly share what should be shared.

---

## 18.5 Permanence and the Future

Information shall endure beyond the people who created it.

The Company shall preserve its significant information — its records, its decisions, its knowledge and its history — so that future generations inherit a complete and trustworthy record of the Company's life. The preservation of information is part of the Company's stewardship of its own memory.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **Information is a strategic asset of the Company. It shall be accurate, secure and permanent — for the Company's decisions, its trust and its future.**
