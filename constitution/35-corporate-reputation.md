# Chapter 35 — Corporate Reputation

*This chapter defines reputation as one of the Company's most valuable long-term assets. The Company's reputation is the accumulated judgement of everyone who has dealt with it — earned slowly, held with care, and lost more quickly than it was built.*

---

## 35.1 Reputation Is a Strategic Asset

The Company's reputation is one of its most valuable long-term assets.

A reputation for quality, integrity and trust is worth more to the Company than any facility or equipment: it is the reason customers choose the Company, partners work with it and communities welcome it. The Company's reputation is built over years and generations, and it shall be protected accordingly.

---

## 35.2 Reputation Is Earned, Not Claimed

The Company's reputation is earned through conduct, not claimed through words.

Reputation is the judgement of others formed over time — from the Company's products, its records, its dealings and its behaviour. The Company shall earn its reputation by being consistently worthy of it, in ways that others can observe and verify.

---

## 35.3 What the Company's Reputation Rests Upon

The Company's reputation rests upon:

- **Quality** — products that meet their promises, consistently;
- **Integrity** — conduct that is honest and dependable;
- **Trustworthiness** — claims supported by evidence;
- **Fair dealing** — honest treatment of people, partners and communities;
- **Stewardship** — responsible care of the land and what is entrusted to the Company;
- **Constancy** — the same standards, in good times and difficult ones.

---

## 35.4 Protecting the Reputation

The Company shall protect its reputation with the care its value demands.

The Company shall:

- uphold its standards in every activity, so that nothing is done that would diminish its good name;
- act promptly and honestly when something threatens its reputation, correcting what is wrong;
- hold its people, partners and supply chain to the standards that protect the reputation;
- decline opportunities that would trade reputation for short-term gain.

The Company recognises that a reputation built over generations can be lost in a single act of dishonesty. No short-term benefit is worth that risk.

---

## 35.5 Reputation Across Generations

The Company's reputation is part of its inheritance.

Each generation receives the reputation its predecessors earned and is responsible for preserving and strengthening it before passing it on. A Company that guards its reputation across generations builds the trust that future generations will depend upon.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **Reputation is one of the Company's most valuable long-term assets — earned through consistent conduct, protected with care, and passed on stronger to each generation.**
