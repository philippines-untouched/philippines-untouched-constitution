# Chapter 40 — Automation

*This chapter defines when automation should be embraced and when human expertise remains essential. The Company automates what automation does better, and keeps human judgement where human judgement matters.*

---

## 40.1 Automation Serves the Company

Automation is a tool of the Company's work — a means of performing tasks reliably and efficiently.

The Company embraces automation where it serves the Company's purpose: improving consistency, preserving evidence, reducing error, freeing people for work that requires judgement, and enabling the Company to operate well at scale.

---

## 40.2 When Automation Is Embraced

The Company shall embrace automation where it:

- **improves consistency** — performing repetitive tasks to the same standard, every time;
- **reduces error** — eliminating the mistakes that human repetition invites;
- **strengthens evidence** — recording activities accurately and completely;
- **frees capability** — releasing people for work that requires their judgement and skill;
- **enables scale** — allowing the Company to grow without loss of standard;
- **serves people** — making the work of the Company's people safer or better.

---

## 40.3 When Human Expertise Remains Essential

The Company shall keep human expertise where it matters most.

Human judgement, care and responsibility remain essential in:

- **decisions of consequence** — where judgement, ethics and accountability are required;
- **relationships** — where trust is built between people;
- **quality and safety** — where care and discernment protect the Company's standards;
- **unforeseen situations** — where automation has not been prepared for what has occurred;
- **stewardship** — where responsibility for outcomes cannot be delegated to a machine.

Automation shall serve these human responsibilities, not replace them.

---

## 40.4 The Discipline of Automation

The Company's automation shall be disciplined:

- **Purposeful** — adopted for the value it creates, never for novelty;
- **Transparent** — understandable in what it does and how it is controlled;
- **Verifiable** — leaving evidence that can be examined and audited;
- **Reversible** — capable of being overridden or undone by people when necessary;
- **Accountable** — with clear human responsibility for automated outcomes;
- **Proportionate** — matching the degree of automation to the importance and predictability of the task.

---

## 40.5 Automation and Human Responsibility

Automation does not remove responsibility; it changes where responsibility is exercised.

The people of the Company remain responsible for the outcomes of automation — for choosing what to automate, for verifying that it performs correctly, and for intervening when it does not. No automation shall diminish the accountability of the Company's people.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **The Company automates what automation does better, and keeps human judgement where judgement matters — with the people of the Company always accountable for the outcomes.**
