# Chapter 32 — GLOBAL G.A.P. IFA Smart V6

*This chapter explains why internationally recognised certification forms part of the Company's strategic direction. Certification is pursued for the substance it represents — not as a marketing badge, but as the disciplined standard of the Company's farming and quality systems.*

---

## 32.1 Certification as Strategic Direction

The Company's strategic direction includes the pursuit of internationally recognised certification — specifically **GLOBAL G.A.P. Integrated Farm Assurance (IFA) Smart Version 6**.

Certification is a major strategic advantage for the Company's international export markets: it demonstrates, through independent verification, that the Company's farming and quality systems meet an internationally recognised standard.

---

## 32.2 Substance, Not Badge

Certification shall not be treated merely as a marketing badge.

The value of certification lies in what it represents: systems that genuinely meet the standard, verified by an independent body. The Company pursues certification so that its standards are real and provable — not so that a certificate can be displayed.

The Company shall design its farming and quality systems around internationally recognised standards from the beginning, so that certification, when obtained, confirms what the Company already does — rather than requiring the Company to begin doing it.

---

## 32.3 What the Standard Requires

GLOBAL G.A.P. IFA Smart V6 certification requires the Company's systems to meet standards including:

- **Food safety** — protection of the safety of products;
- **Traceability** — the ability to follow products from origin to customer;
- **Environmental stewardship** — responsible care for the environment;
- **Biodiversity** — protection of the variety of life;
- **Responsible resource management** — careful use of land, water, energy and materials;
- **Worker welfare** — fair and safe conditions for those who work;
- **Good Agricultural Practices** — disciplined farming to recognised standards;
- **Documented processes** — systems recorded and understood;
- **Verification** — standards checked and evidenced;
- **Continuous improvement** — standards maintained and raised over time.

These requirements align with the Company's own principles; the standard gives them an externally verified form.

---

## 32.4 Certification as a Strategic Objective

Certification is a strategic objective of the Company.

Until certification is formally obtained, it shall be described as a strategic objective — never as an achieved status. The Company shall not imply that it is certified before the certification body has formally granted it.

The Company shall pursue certification honestly: meeting the standard genuinely, preparing thoroughly, and accepting the discipline of independent verification.

---

## 32.5 Certification and the Company's Purpose

Certification serves the Company's purpose.

It strengthens the Company's trust with customers and markets, brings the discipline of international standards into the Company's daily work, and demonstrates the Company's commitment to quality, safety and responsibility in a form the world can verify.

Certification is a means of fulfilling the Company's purpose — a proof of the standards the Company holds, pursued with integrity.

---

## Foundational Principle

> **The Company pursues internationally recognised certification for the substance it represents — designing its systems to meet the standard, and claiming no certification it has not formally earned.**
