# Authoring Principles

**Version:** 1.0 — Ratified
**Status:** Ratified by the authoring foundation — binds every future author and every future amendment
**Owner:** Philippines Untouched OPC

---

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the principles by which the Corporate Governance & Strategy Manual — the Constitution of Philippines Untouched OPC — is written, revised and ratified.

It is a working instrument of the authoring process. It is subordinate to the Constitution it serves. Once ratified, it binds every future author and every future amendment, unless it is itself formally amended in accordance with the same discipline it prescribes.

---

## 2. What the Constitution Is

The Constitution is the highest governing document of the Company. It is:

- **Constitutional** — the authority from which all subordinate documents derive, including Articles of Incorporation, By-Laws, policies, strategies, plans, manuals, procedures, quality systems and standards. Where a conflict exists between the Constitution and a subordinate document, the Constitution prevails.
- **Principle-based, not procedural** — it explains why the Company exists, what it believes, how it governs itself and how decisions should be made. It does not describe how individual tasks are performed.
- **Timeless** — written to remain relevant for decades and generations, regardless of changes in products, technology, markets, ownership or leadership.
- **Strategic, not operational** — it sets direction and standards; implementation belongs to subordinate documents.
- **The Company's own voice** — the enduring expression of what the Company stands for.

---

## 3. What the Constitution Is Not

The Constitution must never become:

- a biography of the founder, or a record of any individual's aspirations;
- a marketing brochure or promotional material;
- a comparison with other organisations;
- a product catalogue;
- a detailed procedure or operating manual;
- a short-term business plan;
- a collection of corporate clichés;
- a reaction to current business trends or public opinion;
- a document whose meaning depends upon any one individual's continued involvement.

The founder's experience may explain how a principle was discovered. The principle itself must stand independently of the founder, the owner, the CEO, the family and every other individual.

---

## 4. The Subject Is the Company

The Company is always the subject of the Constitution. The constitutional question is:

> What is right for Philippines Untouched and consistent with its principles?

The Company is greater than any individual. Ownership, governance, leadership and executive management are distinct responsibilities that may be held by different people at different times. The Constitution must remain true regardless of who holds them.

---

## 5. Language and Voice

The Constitution shall be written in:

- plain language, free of unnecessary jargon;
- a positive voice — defining what the Company stands **for**, not what it stands against;
- a measured tone — precise, calm and confident, never defensive and never adversarial;
- language that distinguishes **principle** from **implementation**, and **strategic objective** from **achieved status**.

The Constitution shall never contain comparisons with other organisations, nor language that diminishes others. The Company strives to be the best it can be through its own standards, not by reference to competitors.

---

## 6. Evidence Over Assertion

The Company believes there is no better quality control than trustworthy evidence preserved over time. The Constitution shall therefore:

- make only claims that can be supported;
- never assert achievements that have not been formally obtained — certification, accreditation and recognition are strategic objectives until they are formally granted;
- prefer verifiable fact over unsupported assertion;
- preserve the record of decisions and their reasons, so that future generations can understand not only what was decided but why.

Technology may change. The principle of trustworthy evidence shall not.

---

## 7. The Future Leader Test

Every chapter of the Constitution shall be tested against this standard:

> A future leader who has never met the founder must be able to read the Constitution and truthfully say: *"I understand what Philippines Untouched is, what it stands for, and what has been entrusted to me."*

A chapter that requires knowledge of the founder, the family, or any individual to be understood is not complete.

---

## 8. Definition of Done

A chapter is complete only when it satisfies all of the following:

- It expresses a meaningful and durable principle.
- It is consistent with the chapters before it.
- It is consistent with the Company's overall philosophy.
- A future leader would understand it.
- It would help someone make a better decision.
- It could remain relevant for decades.
- It strengthens the Company's legacy.
- The language is clear.
- The language is positive rather than reactive.
- It avoids unnecessary comparison with other organisations.
- It distinguishes principle from implementation.

If any of these is not satisfied, the chapter is not complete.

---

## 9. Ratification and Amendment

The Constitution evolves by formal amendment, never by silent change. Every amendment shall:

- preserve the historical record — no version is destroyed or concealed;
- clearly identify the reason for the change;
- state the approving authority;
- identify the sections affected;
- strengthen, rather than weaken, the founding principles.

The principles of the Company remain constant while its strategies, technologies and operations evolve to meet the needs of each generation.

---

## 10. Working Method

The Constitution is developed collaboratively, incrementally and deliberately:

1. The authoring foundation is agreed before substantive chapters are written.
2. Each chapter is drafted, reviewed, refined and ratified in turn.
3. Chapters are cross-referenced and subjected to a complete consistency review before any edition is issued.
4. The repository is treated as institutional memory: meaningful commits, clear messages, permanent history, version tags and formal amendment records.
5. Significant authoring decisions are recorded in the Decision Log, with their reasons, so that future authors can understand why the Constitution took its present form.

Large problems are addressed one meaningful part at a time.

---

## 11. Provenance of Authoring Context

The reasoning behind this Constitution — the authoring brief, planning notes and private deliberations — is preserved as a private institutional record in the Philippines Untouched farm repository, which is the Company's private software platform repository. This public repository holds the Constitution and its authoring framework only.

Future authors seeking the reasoning behind any principle should consult the private context record before proposing change. The distinction is deliberate: the Constitution is public because the Company is accountable to the principles it publishes; the reasoning is private because planning deliberations are not for public consumption.

This decision is recorded in the Decision Log.
