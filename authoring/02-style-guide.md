# Style Guide

**Version:** 1.0 — Ratified
**Status:** Binding — every chapter of the Constitution shall conform to these conventions

---

## 1. Constitutional Register

The Constitution speaks in the voice of the Company itself — not an individual, not a committee, not a marketing department.

| Register | Example |
|---|---|
| **Constitutional** | *"The Company shall maintain accurate records of every significant decision."* |
| **Not procedural** | ❌ *"Records should be saved in the document management system."* |
| **Not promotional** | ❌ *"We pride ourselves on our world-class record-keeping."* |

The tone is precise, calm and confident — never defensive, never adversarial, never boastful.

---

## 2. Voice

Write in the **positive voice** — define what the Company stands **for**, not what it stands against.

| Positive (preferred) | Negative (avoid) |
|---|---|
| *"The Company seeks to build enduring value."* | *"The Company rejects short-termism."* |
| *"Quality is prioritised over short-term profit."* | *"The Company will not sacrifice quality for money."* |

This rule admits exceptions only when the negative statement is necessary to draw a clear boundary — for example, defining what the Constitution is not in the Front Matter. Even then, the primary definition should be positive.

---

## 3. The Constitutional "Shall"

The word **shall** expresses a constitutional obligation. Use it sparingly and only for binding requirements.

- **Shall** — a binding obligation that must be fulfilled.
- **Should** — a strong recommendation, deviation requires documented justification.
- **May** — discretionary permission.
- **Will not / Must not** — prohibitions (use rarely; prefer positive expression).

**Examples:**
- *"The Company shall maintain a complete and permanent record of every amendment to this Constitution."*
- *"The Company should seek to improve the health of the land with each generation."*
- *"The Board may delegate authority to the CEO, but accountability shall remain with the Board."*

---

## 4. The Company and Its Pronouns

The Company is referred to as **the Company** (capitalised). Use the third-person singular **it** / **its**.

- *"The Company exists to create enduring value. **It** fulfils **its** purpose through..."*

Never use **we** / **us** / **our** in the constitutional body. The Founder's Foreword (0.5) is the one exception — it may use personal voice.

---

## 5. Capitalisation

The following terms are capitalised when referring to the Company and its Constitution:

| Term | Rule |
|---|---|
| **the Company** | Always capitalised — refers to Philippines Untouched OPC |
| **the Constitution** | Always capitalised — refers to this document |
| **the Manual** | Alternative reference to the Constitution (capitalised) |
| **the Board** | When referring to the Company's Board of Directors |
| **the Founder** | Only in the Founder's Foreword and historical context |
| **Founding Principles** | Capitalised when referring to the 13 principles (Chapter 5) |
| **Part / Chapter / Appendix** | Capitalised when used as a formal reference: *"as defined in Chapter 7"* |

Do not capitalise common nouns for stylistic emphasis. Do not capitalise **director**, **CEO**, **officer** when used generically.

---

## 6. Headings

### Part headings
```
# Part I — Identity
```

### Chapter headings
```
## Chapter 1 — Purpose
```

### Section headings within a chapter
```
### 1.1 The Purpose of the Company
```

### Subsection headings
```
#### 1.1.1 Primary Purpose
```

Headings use **Title Case**. No full stop at the end of a heading.

Each chapter begins with a one-paragraph preamble (italicised or in plain text) that summarises the chapter's purpose, followed by a rule.

---

## 7. Lists

Use bullet lists for unordered items and numbered lists for sequential or ranked items.

**Bullet lists:**
- Each item begins with a capital letter.
- No punctuation at the end of list items unless the item is a complete sentence.
- If any item is a complete sentence, all items are complete sentences with full stops.

**Numbered lists:**
1. Use for sequences, priorities, or criteria (e.g., the Definition of Done in Authoring Principles §8).
2. Consistent format within each list.

---

## 8. Quotations

- The Constitution may quote the Founder in the Founder's Foreword (0.5) only.
- Within chapters, use italics or blockquotes for emphasised principles. Avoid quotation marks for constitutional statements.
- Principles expressed as maxims should use blockquote format:
  > *"Quality shall ALWAYS be considered ahead of profit."*

---

## 9. Cross-References

Cross-reference formally:

- *"as established in Chapter 5 (Founding Principles)"*
- *"in accordance with Section 3.2 of this Chapter"*
- *"as defined in Appendix A (Glossary)"*

Do not use relative references (*"as described above"*, *"see later chapter"*). Every reference shall be explicit.

---

## 10. Terminology Consistency

Once a term is defined, use it consistently throughout the Constitution. Do not vary for stylistic reasons.

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| **the Company** | the organisation, the business, the enterprise, the firm |
| **the Constitution** | the manual, the document, the book, the governance manual |
| **Founding Principles** | core principles, fundamental principles, key beliefs |
| **stewardship** | trusteeship, guardianship, custodianship |

The Glossary (Appendix A) is the authoritative source for defined terms.

---

## 11. Numbers, Dates and Measurements

- Numbers one through ten: spell out. Numbers 11 and above: numerals.
- Years: four digits (*2025*, not *'25*).
- Dates: *"28 February 2025"* (day month year, no comma).
- Measurements: use metric (kg, ha, m, °C). Spell out on first use with abbreviation in parentheses.

---

## 12. International English

The Constitution shall be written in **International English**, using Commonwealth spelling conventions:

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| organisation | organization |
| recognised | recognized |
| prioritise | prioritize |
| labour | labor |
| defence | defense |
| licence (noun), license (verb) | — |

Exception: the word **Manual** follows the authoring brief's established usage and is capitalised. Proper names (GLOBAL G.A.P., Philippines Untouched) retain their official spelling.

---

## 13. Amendments and Version Markers

Every amendment shall be clearly identified:
- A revision history entry (0.3).
- Change markers or sidebars in the text where practical.
- A reference to the Decision Log entry that records the reason for the change.

The Constitution never conceals its history. No version is destroyed.

---

## 14. Acronyms and Initialisms

- Spell out on first use in each Part: *"Good Agricultural Practices (GAP)"*.
- Once defined, use the short form consistently.
- Avoid unnecessary acronyms. If a term appears fewer than three times, do not introduce an acronym.

---

## 15. Principles vs Implementation

The Constitution distinguishes clearly between principle and implementation.

- **Principles** use the constitutional imperative (*shall*, continuous present tense).
- **Examples or context** are marked as illustrative: *"For example..."* or *"This may include..."*
- Never include specific technology names, product SKUs, software packages, or vendor names in the constitutional body.
