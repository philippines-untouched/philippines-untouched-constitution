# Decision Log

**Purpose:** This log records significant authoring decisions and their reasons, so that future generations can understand not only what the Constitution says, but why it took its present form. Every entry preserves the reasoning, the alternative considered, and the decision.

---

## Entry 001 — Provenance of Authoring Context

- **Date:** At authoring foundation establishment
- **Decision:** The reasoning behind this Constitution — the authoring brief, planning notes and private deliberations — is preserved as a private institutional record in the Philippines Untouched farm repository (private). This public repository holds the Constitution and its authoring framework only.
- **Reason:** The Constitution is public because the Company is accountable to the principles it publishes. The planning deliberations are private because they contain personal and strategic material not intended for public consumption. Both records are versioned institutional memory; neither is lost.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  1. Publishing the authoring brief in this public repository — rejected: would expose private planning material and contradict the principle that the Constitution is not about any individual.
  2. Keeping the reasoning only in personal notes — rejected: not versioned, not institutional, at risk of loss.
- **Status:** Ratified by the authoring foundation.

## Entry 002 — Choice of Public License

- **Date:** At authoring foundation establishment
- **Decision:** The Constitution is licensed under **Creative Commons Attribution-NoDerivatives 4.0 International (CC BY-ND 4.0)**.
- **Reason:** The Company is accountable to the principles it publishes and the Constitution is intended to be freely readable and shareable by anyone — customers, partners, certification bodies, regulators, the public. CC BY-ND 4.0 permits unlimited sharing and redistribution (including commercial use) with attribution, while prohibiting third parties from publishing altered or derivative versions. This protects the integrity of the constitutional text: the public must always be able to read what the Company actually committed to, not a modified version. The "NoDerivatives" term is consistent with the authoring principle that the Constitution evolves only by formal amendment, never by silent change.
- **Alternatives considered:**
  1. **CC BY 4.0** (attribution only, derivatives allowed) — rejected: would permit third parties to publish modified versions, undermining the principle that only the Company's formal amendment process may change the Constitution.
  2. **CC0 1.0** (public domain dedication) — rejected: would waive all rights and attribution, inconsistent with the institutional authorship principle.
  3. **All rights reserved** — rejected: contrary to the public transparency principle; the Constitution should be freely readable and shareable, not restricted.
  4. **No license** — rejected: would create legal uncertainty; third parties would have no clear permissions.
- **Status:** Ratified by the authoring foundation.
